# What this user notices

The same person reviews every painting, and their taste has been consistent across the medieval painting, the ink era and the city era. Each note below is in their words, followed by the general rule it taught. Read this once before a new picture: most of their notes could have been predicted.

## How they review

- They zoom in. "If I zoom in, it's obvious." Detail the medium smears, a window cut in half, a hairline gap: they will find it.
- They judge the medium and the tradition before the subject. "This is an oil painting we're talking about … the accolade by edmund leighton type of art?" A correct subject in the wrong tradition fails.
- They often can't name the fault. "I don't know it just looks weird how the city is so far away from the road." When the note is vague, look for a broken space or scale, not a detail.
- They say when something is fine, and they still expect it refined. "Looks fine … just needs some more refinement with the new painting skill." Run the full review on accepted work too.
- They want ambition. "Be braver and bolder, you are capable of making wonderful things." A bigger build is welcome when it serves the picture. "If you need a program to work better tell me, I'll install it for you."

## Their notes and the rules behind them

| Their words | The rule |
|---|---|
| "it looks like a kid drew them, or it's a bit low effort rather" | Flat fills, even outlines, primitive shapes, regular spacing and evenly spread detail are rejected on sight. |
| "meh, the medieval art should be a renaissance art instead" | Confirm the art tradition with named examples before drawing. |
| "tsushima is based on japan not china" | Paint the real place's terrain in its own culture's tradition, not a neighbour's. |
| "the proportions are way off" | Eyeballed figures fail. Measure in real units. |
| "you can reference anatomy measurements and armor measurements along with any biography" | Every size should trace to a source, and the source should be named. |
| "have henry in full armor … similar to the reference image" | Their reference images are the spec. |
| "make henry pose a bit more to the front" (with an image) | Poses are judged against references, including the body's facing. |
| "it looks like he's having a hard time resting there" | Mood comes from weight and contact. |
| "the cars go past the trees so there's a depth issue here" | Draw order must match depth along every path a moving object takes. |
| "the car does not look like a car" | Machines need measured proportions and the few marks that identify them. |
| "the aesthetic of the car won't fit when it transitions to cyberpunk era" | When an era changes, every era-bound object changes with it. Recolouring is not enough. |
| "road and reflection needs a bit of work" | Reflections need a surface under their source, drawn the way that surface breaks light. |
| "don't be specific to the task" (about this skill) | Lessons are recorded as general rules, with a short example, not as a story. |
