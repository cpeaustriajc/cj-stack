# The four Diátaxis modes

Two questions pick the mode. Does the content inform action (doing) or understanding (thinking)?
Does it serve learning or work? Use the compass on a whole document or on one sentence.

**Tutorial: learning by doing.** The learner's success is your job, not theirs. Open by saying
what the learner will build, not what they will "learn". Every step produces a visible result,
early and often. Tell them what they should see: the expected output, the prompt change, the
log line. Cut explanation to one clause and a link. Stay concrete. Write as "we", in commands:
"First, do x. Now, do y."

**How-to: steps to a goal.** Solve a problem a person has, not an operation the machine can do.
Assume competence and skip teaching. Give only the actions: no digressions, no background, no
completeness for its own sake. Link those instead. Allow forks and judgment: "If you want x, do
y." Name the guide by the task: "How to calibrate the radar array", not "Radar array
calibration".

**Reference: facts for lookup.** Only describe. No instruction, no persuasion, no opinion. Be
dry, complete and sure. State facts, options, limits and errors with no hedging. Mirror the
structure of the thing you describe, so readers can move through the code and the docs together.
Put material where readers expect it. Generate it from the code where you can.

**Explanation: understanding and why.** Write about one bounded topic that a reader can follow
away from the product. Each title must make sense with an implicit "About..." in front. Anchor
on a real "why" question. Give context: design decisions, history, constraints and alternatives.
Opinion is allowed here and nowhere else. Weigh the trade-offs and say what you make of them.

Do not mix modes: no reference tables inside a tutorial, no tutorial steps inside reference, no
argument inside a how-to. Split and link instead.
