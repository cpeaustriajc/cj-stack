# Armour, costume and how to research them

What was learned getting Henry's kit right (Bohemia, 1403, after the KCD2 cover art), and a method for costume in other periods. Sizes are in centimetres. Museum figures come from the Met's Chalcis collection and from reproduction makers' spec pages; guesses are marked.

## European kit about 1390-1420

**Bascinet with visor.**
- **Skull:** tall and ogival, with the apex toward the rear and a faint keel down the front and back. The back falls almost straight to the nape. About 34 high, 21.6 wide and 23.5 deep (Met Chalcis pieces, 29.158.45 and .46).
- **Visor types:**
  - klappvisor: one hinge at the brow; mostly German, rarer in Italy after about 1380
  - houndskull: a long pointed snout
  - rounded or bow-fronted visor on two side hinges: a central vertical ridge, and one sight slit across or **two sights**, one either side of the ridge
- **Breaths:** small round holes, staggered in rows below the sights. Some visors have them on both sides, others only on the right (the left side was kept solid against a lance).
- **Visor size:** the Met's Chalcis visor measures 16.5 high, 19.7 wide and 17.8 deep. Mounted, it projects about 5-6 beyond the face. It is not a beak.
- **Hinges and rim:** pinned hinge plates at the temples. Along the lower rim of the skull runs a leather band or vervelles (staples) that carries the aventail.
- **Aventail:** a mail cape hanging from the rim. It drapes over the shoulders and upper chest like a short mantle, about 15-18 at the front and 20-25 at the sides and back. Rings are about 0.8-1 across.

**Breastplate.**
- Globose (a rounded "pigeon breast", Churburg type), about 36 from the neck opening to the natural waist.
- A V-shaped stop-rib below the neck, turned edges at the neck and armholes, and leather straps over the shoulders.
- The lower edge stops at the natural waist. The KCD2 cover wears it over a red doublet.

**Cloth (KCD2 cover kit).**
- A red quilted arming doublet. Long dagged strips hang from the shoulders over the upper arms.
- A dagged red skirt reaches about mid-thigh, with mail showing through the slits and below the hem.
- A black waist belt with a long hanging strap end, and a brown hip belt.

**Arms.**
- The rerebrace covers the lower two-thirds of the upper arm.
- The couter has a fan- or heart-shaped wing on the outside of the elbow.
- The vambrace is closed and tapers to the wrist.
- Hourglass gauntlets have a flared cuff with a point on the outer side, a domed plate over the back of the hand, a knuckle lame, and finger scales on leather.

**Legs.**
- The cuisse covers the front of the thigh.
- The poleyn has a side wing.
- The greave is closed, with a hinge on the outside and a calf bulge.
- Sabatons are laminated, with lames on top only, and moderately pointed around 1400. In the cover art the legs read darker than the polished torso.

**Sword (the reforged sword Martin made for Sir Radzig).**
- Blade 89, about 5.3 wide at the forte, with a fuller in its upper third.
- An arched cross about 24 across, a grip of about 22, and a wheel pommel about 5.6 across.
- Total about 120. Leaning against a tree beside a seated man, it stands taller than his head.

## Layering and fit

Work from the body outward: body, padded doublet, mail, plate, then outer cloth and belts. Each layer sits outside the one beneath it wherever they overlap:
- Cloth under plate has a smaller radius than the plate over it, or the plate vanishes inside the sleeve.
- Plate sits 1-2 outside the padded body.
- Mail drapes over padded shoulders, so the shoulder shape has to be part of the drape.
- Straps and belts sit about 0.4 outside whatever they lie on.

## Mistakes made

- **Size.** The helmet and pauldron were oversized until they were checked against museum dimensions. Measure every piece.
- **Wrong visor.** A pointed houndskull was drawn when the references showed a blunt bow-fronted visor. Read the references' visor type, slit count and breaths layout.
- **Date.** Milanese harness belongs to about 1450, and KCD2's endgame set is the "Noble" set. Say so when the request mixes dates, then follow the user.
- **Armour ignoring the outfit.** The cover art shows a red dagged doublet over the plate and no pauldrons. Copying a generic museum harness ignored it.
- **Mail.** Fine rings smeared into a flat slab after the oil pass. Draw mail at a ring size the medium keeps, and drape it like heavy cloth.

## Researching costume for another period

Brief a `researcher` agent for shapes and sizes, not history essays:
1. Name each piece with its period term, its shape in plain words, and its size in centimetres from a museum or maker source, with a URL.
2. Give the layering order, and say what shows at the neck, shoulders, wrists and hem.
3. Say what the game's own art shows (cover art, key art, in-game stills) and where it departs from history.
4. Mark anything unverified as UNVERIFIED.

Worked starting points, from game-canon pictures:
- **Kamakura Japan, 1274 (Ghost of Tsushima).** Ō-yoroi: lamellar of kozane laced in coloured silk, large square sode shoulder boards, a kabuto with fukigaeshi (turned-back wings), sometimes kuwagata horns, and a menpō or hōate face guard. The Tokyo National Museum and the Met's Japanese collection publish dimensions. Check Jin Sakai's canon armour sets against game art.
- **Late Sengoku (Sekiro).** Wolf wears a shinobi's practical kit. Check the Shinobi Prosthetic, the Kusabimaru (a katana with a blade of about 70) and his tattered haori and scarf against game art; canon beats history here.
- **Edo period (Onimusha: Way of the Sword).** Musashi and the Oni Gauntlet come from game key art. Tosei-gusoku plate-and-lame armour for any samurai foes.
- **Night City and Los Santos.** Read clothing and cars from game stills. Car proportions (wheelbase, wheel diameter) are as checkable as armour sizes.
