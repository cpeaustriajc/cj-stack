# Scout

Finding goes wrong in a few predictable ways. The idea is a feature the model labs will ship
next quarter. It solves a problem nobody pays to solve. It is clever to build and has no way to
reach a buyer, so the first clone with an audience wins. Or it is something the buyer could now
build privately with an agent in a weekend. This skill looks for demand first and filters for
those before anything reaches `vision`.

## 1. Start from what I have

Ask, in one round, before any method:

- **What I have that others don't**: a problem I hit weekly, a job or industry I know from the
  inside, a community I'm part of, an audience, a skill, a domain name, data.
- **What I want from it**: money (how much, how soon), learning, or joy. Personal software is a
  fine answer; then `vision`'s "for me alone" questions apply and the filters below matter less.
- **How much time**: evenings for a month, or a year of weekends.

The best ideas sit on the first answer. An idea with no edge of mine is a race against everyone
with the same agent.

## 2. Run one or two methods

Each fits an afternoon. Pick the ones my edge points at; look things up rather than asking me.

- **My own itch.** List the manual tasks I repeated three or more times in the last month and the
  paid tools that annoyed me. A task I keep rebuilding for myself is the strongest signal there is.
- **Follow the money.** Where do people already pay for this outcome, before any AI: invoices,
  freelancers, a clunky incumbent, a published revenue figure in a directory of verified startup
  revenue. Small proven earners are better than big empty markets. Then ask what niche, locale
  or angle they leave open.
- **Complaints about incumbents.** In one narrow category, read 50 two- and three-star reviews
  of the paying tools. Tag each by theme and count; keep the themes that repeat across two or more
  competitors.
- **A community's own words.** Read a week of a community's top threads I belong to or can join.
  List problems in their words, and work they pay someone to do by hand.
- **Search demand.** A purchase people already search for, with a modifier that repeats (a city,
  an industry, a use) so one product can answer many searches.
- **An ecosystem's gaps.** The "missing feature" threads and low reviews in a marketplace with
  built-in discovery: a store's apps, a platform's plugins. A small paid add-on is a good first
  step before a full product.
- **Unbundle.** One feature of a big tool that a niche uses alone, done better for them.

Write each candidate as one line: who, the painful job, what they pay or do now, and the method
that found it.

## 3. Filter what a clone can't take

Score each candidate yes, no or unknown on each line; an unknown that decides it becomes a thing
to look up or a test, not a guess.

1. **People already pay** for this outcome, with money or with a workaround they put up with.
2. **A painkiller**: they would buy it this week, not someday. It can have a buy button on day
   one.
3. **I can reach them**: an audience, a community, search pages, a marketplace listing or a sales
   motion I own or can build. If not, a copy with distribution wins.
4. **My edge is knowledge, not code**: I am the user, or I know the work from the inside.
5. **Something a copy can't take quickly**: data or history that accrues per customer, a
   workflow it sits inside, integrations that are hard to get, trust, a licence or compliance
   track record, a community, a brand.
6. **Small enough that big players won't bother**, and big enough to pay for my time.
7. **Neither the buyer nor a model lab would build it themselves.** A thin tool over
   open-source parts, or a single model call with a form in front, fails this: buyers build the
   first in a weekend and labs absorb the second. Ongoing operations, infrastructure, compliance
   or taste pass it.
8. **Worth it even if copied**: it pays back in weeks, grows my audience either way, or teaches
   me what I wanted to learn.

## 4. Red flags

Beyond a failed filter, say these plainly when a candidate shows them:

- A tool for other builders whose only channel is search, the channel shrinking fastest.
- A free tool or a waitlist as the plan to validate; validation is someone paying.
- Agents and automation before a single user.
- A complex product as the first one, when an add-on or a one-time sale would teach the same.
- Sharing a viral idea's numbers before owning its distribution: it invites the copies.

## 5. Hand over

Show the candidates as a table: the eight filters, the red flags, and a one-line verdict each
(pursue, test first, or drop). Recommend one, or none if none clears the filters; finding nothing
worth building is a good result. On my pick, hand it to `vision` with the evidence gathered, so
its worth-it round starts from facts, not from scratch.
